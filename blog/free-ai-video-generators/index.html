Free AI Video Generators: What Is Actually Free?

A useful free plan should help you make something. Here is how to read the credit allowance, plan a first shot and understand what you can actually export.

By tetricaiPublished / checked: November 27, 2026Documentation-based guide

The short answer: Wizstar offers a 20-credit free entry, Runway lists 125 one-time free credits, and Pika’s current Free plan lists zero monthly credits. “Free account,” “free trial” and “recurring free generation” describe different things.

Official sources checked November 27, 2026. This is a documentation-based guide; no generated-output tests or performance measurements are claimed.

Wizstar makes a welcoming starting point

Wizstar is appealing if you want to explore video creation alongside image and other creative tools in one workspace. Its official homepage advertises 20 free credits with no credit card required. That is a concrete invitation to try the platform, rather than an invitation to subscribe before seeing the workflow.

One particularly useful detail is the credit estimator on its pricing page. You can choose a model, duration and quantity to understand the planned spend. For a beginner, that makes the relationship between a creative choice and its credit cost easier to see. The same workspace also lists text-, image- and reference-driven video workflows, giving you more than one way to begin an idea.

The 20-credit offer is a free trial allowance. The public pages do not establish a recurring 20-credit refill for a free account. They also do not establish a universal number of finished videos from those credits: model choice and output settings matter. Check which generation is available to your account and its displayed cost before submitting it.

A lovely creative idea: start from your own reference

Wizstar’s in-house R1 reference-to-video model offers a clear creative path: provide at least one reference image, then describe the shot. The published specifications list up to nine references, 5–15-second videos at 720p, vertical or horizontal framing, and native audio generated with the picture. These are documented capabilities, rather than results measured for this article.

That is an inviting format for a small product scene, an animated character or a playful social clip. A reference gives you a subject to build around; a short prompt can then focus on one action and one camera movement. For example, you could use an image you own of a toy robot and ask for a gentle turn toward the camera under warm desk lighting. This is a suggested prompt exercise, not a demonstrated output.

There is also a separate R1 unlimited launch offer. The model page explains that it is for eligible members and depends on the subscription plan, with duration or active-subscription conditions. It covers R1 at the listed settings, and tasks may wait longer at busy times. Treat that as a membership offer; it is separate from the 20-credit free entry. The generator’s current settings and your account’s eligibility are the place to confirm access.

Three different meanings of “free”

The headline price is only one part of the story. These current official pages describe three different starting budgets.

A credit is an internal unit, so 125 credits on one service cannot be compared directly with 20 on another. Nor does a model appearing somewhere on a platform guarantee that its free plan includes that model. Check the actual free-account selector and generation cost.

Pika’s current wording is a good reminder to open the live plan page rather than trust an older “free tools” roundup. A $0 account can still require a separate purchase before generating. Likewise, watermark-free output and permission for commercial use are separate questions.

Spend the trial on one small idea

Before opening the generator, write down a subject, an action and a framing choice. “A toy robot turns toward the camera, warm desk lighting, a steady close-up” is easier to evaluate than a request for a complete advertisement with multiple scenes.

  1. Prepare your inputs. Use a reference image you own or have permission to upload. Decide whether you need portrait or landscape framing.
  2. Inspect the cost. Select the model, shortest suitable supported duration and one output. Read the displayed credit requirement before generating.
  3. Review the important detail. Did the subject stay recognizable? Did the intended action happen? Is any generated sound useful?
  4. Change one thing. Adjust the action or camera instruction rather than rewriting everything. Keep enough credits to compare a second attempt if your budget allows.

If a job costs c credits and your usable balance is b, the theoretical upper bound is the whole-number part of b ÷ c. That only helps after checking the actual cost and charging rules. It does not predict how many attempts will produce a clip you want to keep.

Check the final file, not just the preview

A playable preview is encouraging, but the download is what you will actually use. Before committing time to a workflow, check four things in the current plan and export screen:

  • Watermark: whether your selected export includes branding, and whether removing it requires a different plan.
  • Resolution and duration: the delivered file’s limits for that model and account.
  • Usage rights: whether the intended personal or commercial use is allowed. An unwatermarked file does not answer that question.
  • Budget rules: whether credits renew, expire or are charged again for another variation.

The Wizstar and Runway free allowances above do not establish every export condition for every model. Confirm those conditions in the product before planning a client deliverable. For a first creative experiment, Wizstar’s card-free entry, varied starting inputs and visible estimator make a friendly combination: you can arrive with a small idea and understand the choices in front of you.
